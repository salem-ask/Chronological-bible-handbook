The Chronological Bible Reading Plan

Read the whole Bible in 365 days, in the order the events happened.

The reading plan helps you put the chronological approach into practice throughout the year. Instead of reading the books of the Bible one after another in their usual order, you follow the story as it unfolded: from Creation, through the patriarchs, the Exodus, the kings and the prophets, the exile and the return, to the life of Jesus, the early Church and the Book of Revelation.

How to use this plan

1

One reading each day. There are 365 daily readings covering every chapter of the Bible once. You can start on any date.

2

About 15 minutes a day. The readings were divided by the length of the King James Version text, so each day has a similar amount to read (about 2,200 words on average).

3

Tick the box when you finish a reading. If you miss a day, simply continue from the next unticked reading.

4

Use the Handbook alongside. When a book appears for the first time, the plan points you to its presentation in the Chronological Bible Handbook.

Handbook pages. Each book's page number in the Handbook is listed in the Handbook's chronological summary tables. Write it once in the book list on the next page, and next to the book on the day it first appears in the plan (___).

How the readings are arranged

  • The historical books form the backbone of the plan. Parallel accounts, such as Samuel and Kings with Chronicles, are read side by side.
  • The prophets are read with the reigns and events named at the start of their books, following the approximate dates on the Handbook's biblical timeline.
  • Psalms whose titles mention an event in David's life are read with that event. Psalms that speak of the destruction of Jerusalem or the return from exile are read in those periods. The other psalms are read in their Bible order during the time of David and Solomon.
  • The four Gospels are read side by side, period by period. Paul's letters are read at the points in Acts where they were most probably written.

The dates of some books and passages are uncertain, and Bible scholars place some of them differently (for example Job, several psalms, James, Galatians and Hebrews). This plan follows one reasonable arrangement; it is a reading aid, not a final ruling on dates. Bible references follow the King James Version (KJV).
